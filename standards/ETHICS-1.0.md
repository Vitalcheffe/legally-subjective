# ETHICS-1.0 — Public Vocabulary Charter

**Ratified 2026-08-27, on order of the internal audit LS-AUDIT-001 (injonction 11).**
This charter governs every word this project prints next to a named,
living, sitting judge. It is part of the standard suite: violating it is a
defect, not a style choice.

---

## 1. The principle

The project measures and publishes facts about public officials. A fact is
defensible; a verdict is not ours to give. Therefore:

> **No label placed next to a named judge may imply a deficiency, a virtue,
> a character trait, or a disposition — unless the public record itself
> states it.**

We count divergences, rates, densities, and intervals. We do not grade
souls.

## 2. The capture test (mandatory before shipping any label)

A journalist photographs the screen. The caption writes itself. Ask:

1. **Torn from its context** — page, disclaimer, methodology, interval —
   does the label remain factual?
2. Would the judge themselves, reading it, recognize a measured fact —
   even an unflattering one — rather than an editorial judgment?
3. Does the label describe **what happened** (a divergence, a rate) or
   **what someone is** (a temperament, a failing)?

If any answer fails, the label does not ship.

## 3. Banned and prescribed vocabulary

| Banned (normative) | Prescribed (factual) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| blind spot | **divergence profile** | a divergence is a fact; a blind spot is a verdict |
| soft on crime / lenient | lower reversal share, with n and ± | "lenient" presumes a right answer we do not have |
| tough / severe / harsh | higher dissent rate; higher petitioner-alignment rate | severity is a moral scale, not a count |
| activist / restrained | (do not use; cite the measured axis instead) | legally loaded terms owned by a political fight |
| predictable / erratic | dissent predictability (AUC), with its interval | "erratic" imputes irrationality |
| empathetic / cold | not measurable from votes — do not print | the record cannot support it |
| "the judge who ruins lives" style hooks | "the door with the lowest agreement rate" | drama belongs to the draw, not to the person |

The words *minority*, *majority*, *petitioner*, *align*, *diverge*,
*rate*, *share*, *count*, *interval* are the working vocabulary. Nearly
everything the project needs to say can be said with those.

## 4. Rules of application

1. **Every public percentage carries its n and its ±** (audit injonction 1).
   A bare number next to a name is the most dangerous object this project
   can ship.
2. **Named judges get named with title and respect** on first mention in
   prose (e.g., "Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson"); stamps and data tables
   may use surnames.
3. **Short records are flagged as short records** (audit injonction 5) —
   a smaller n is a measurement condition, never an insinuation about the
   person.
4. **The project never predicts a case** and never prints anything that
   reads as one. Simulation outputs, when they exist, are labeled
   "simulation" per the roadmap.
5. **Corrections are public**: when a label is found violating this
   charter, the fix ships with a revision note (see LS-1.0 §4,
   immutability) — the first use was the `ci95` → `rank_band` renaming of
   2026-08-27.

## 5. Scope

This charter binds: the public site, the research article, the README, the
video script, social posts, and every artifact that quotes a judge's name
with a number. Where a historical archived document violates it, the
document is annotated, not silently rewritten — the same discipline we
demand of courts.
