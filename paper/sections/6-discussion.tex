\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Who votes beats what the case is about}

The M3a null result is a negative finding with a positive reading.
The failure of three model families to convert issue areas,
lower-court dispositions, terms, and argument lengths into votes
tells us that the mapping from case facts to outcomes is either
unstable across time or not expressible in these coarse codes. The
ablation sharpens this: keeping issue area actively hurt
generalization, which is the signature of a signal that shifted
between the training and evaluation windows---the mix and meaning
of ``economic activity'' cases in OT2015 is not the mix of OT2022.
Justice identity, by contrast, travels intact across the split,
because it is a property of the voter, not of the era.

This is a modern, pre-registered echo of the attitudinal model's
central claim~\citep{segal2002}, obtained with none of its
theoretical apparatus: one revealed-ideology bit per justice
carries more predictive weight than the entire structured case
file. It also explains, mechanically, why the earlier literature's
headlines sit above our bar. \citet{katz2017} report 71.9\% at the
vote level---but over 1816--2013, an era range containing long
stretches of 80--90\% unanimity that no modern model enjoys; on the
modern, 5--4-heavy window, the honest point of comparison is
closer to our setting, and the 63.7\% bar is what an
ideology-only adversary achieves when unanimity is gone.

\subsection{The sealed exam delivered the designed outcome}

Had a structured challenger cleared the bar, the persona experiment
would have been partially redundant: some of the textual signal
would already be recoverable from codes. The M3a null did the
opposite---it made the text conditions the critical path---and the
seal has now been broken on them, exactly once, under the
pre-registered protocol. The persona condition was the strongest
version of that probe: any residual textual signal would have to be
\emph{individual} and \emph{prospective}, and the stylometry
lineage~\citep{mosteller1964,juola2006} gave the instrument a
principled footing, since style is known to be persistent and hard
to fake. The result is the outcome the protocol was built to
deliver cleanly, not a failure state. On the primary
endpoint---case-direction accuracy on the fifty hash-frozen 5--4
decisions---the persona scores 59.2\% against the zero-shot's
61.2\%, three discordant cases apart (exact McNemar, $p=1.00$); the
point estimate sits two points on the wrong side of zero, and the
interval admits no honest reading except no detectable gain. The
retrieval condition fares worse (53.1\%). On the transparent
window, the same comparison had shown a $+4.9$-point edge for the
persona, not significant; on the sealed exam the sign flips.
Case-level deltas of this magnitude are noise-dominated at these
sample sizes---which is precisely why the seal, and the one-shot
pre-registered test it protects, existed.

The re-fit ideological prior sharpens the reading from the other
side. On the same one-vote-margin decisions it collapses to a coin
flip at case level (50.0\%), while at vote level it still dominates
every language condition (75.9\% against 50--60\%). Close cases
remove exactly the case-level margin that ideology exploits
elsewhere, and none of the conditions---statistical rule or
language model---does much beyond the always-conservative majority
class. To our knowledge, no public test of this question has combined
a sealed holdout, single scoring, and audited anti-memorization
checks; under that combination, at this scale, the result is null. We
phrase the scope exactly: the claim is not that all textual signal is
absent---richer models, other windows, or full-dossier encodings
may yet find some---it is that the strongest individual-prospective
probe, run under the strongest integrity constraints we could
construct, found none.

\subsection{Why: the persona learns the voice, not the vote}

A null invites the question the mechanistic half of the experiment
was pre-registered to answer: what did personalization actually do
to the model? Three probes converge on one answer. First, it moved
votes without moving outcomes: thirty-seven discordant votes
against the zero-shot on the sealed set, a nominal vote-level edge
($p=.047$, secondary, unadjusted) that never converts into a single
case-level gain. Second, it moved the model's ideological posture
rather than its judgment: on the sealed exam every simulated
justice votes more conservatively than the justice it imitates, and
on the transparent window, where the arms decompose, the
biographical prompt does most of the moving---it lifts the
simulated liberal seats substantially toward their justices'
records (Sotomayor $+0.39$) while barely moving the conservative
ones---while the fine-tuned adapter's marginal contribution on top
of the prompt never exceeds a tenth and is negative for its two
pre-registered targets. Identity injected in the prompt is not
behavior learned by the adapter. Third, the accounts that would
have made persona quality legible fail on contact with the data:
the pre-registered stylistic story---that the most distinctive pens
yield the best personas---is falsified ($\rho=-0.72$, the sign
opposite the prediction), with fidelity instead tracking each
justice's alignment with the base model's conservative default; and
the auto-coherence probe shows personas misvoting on the very
training cases whose texts they absorbed, the relationship inverted
for four of the seven adapters. The adapter has seen the justice's
words; it did not learn the vote that produced them.

The surface style transfers; the decision function does not. What
personalization learned is the voice, and what prediction needed
was the vote---and on this evidence the two are separable: a model
can absorb a justice's prose without absorbing anything that
predicts that justice's hardest calls. This is the sense in which
the null is informative rather than empty. It separates two things
the literature on judicial prediction and the literature on
stylometry had reason to believe were entangled, and it locates the
predictable component of a close decision in who the justice is,
not in how the justice writes.
